Sentences like "the room was quiet" or "they looked tired" are easy to read, but the scene doesn't stay with you. An adjective hands over the conclusion first, which leaves the reader less room to imagine. Put an action in its place and the same message comes through far more sharply. Pick just one adjective in something you wrote and swap it for an action.

## Why an action stays longer than an adjective

You can see an action. "Tired" is an explanation, but "stood at the foot of the stairs holding the railing for a while" is a scene. The reader sees the action and works out "tired" without being told. What a reader works out tends to be remembered longer than what they are told.

## Three steps to swap an adjective for an action

1. **Pick one adjective or feeling word.** Any word that names a state will do: "quiet," "worn out," "glad."
2. **Recall what the body did in that state.** Think about what the hands, feet, eyes or voice did. Catching one detail is enough.
3. **Delete the adjective and write that action in one sentence.** The word you started with should not appear again.

## Example: the quiet evening

Say the first sentence is:

> The house was quiet that evening.

Recall what the body did and rewrite:

> All evening I could hear the refrigerator humming. I set my chopsticks down slowly, afraid they would click.

The word "quiet" is gone, yet you can feel how quiet it was.

## Common stumbling blocks

- **Trying to remove every adjective.** Changing one or two per scene is enough to change the writing. Changing all of them leaves it breathless.
- **Writing the action too long.** One sentence is plenty. A long action pulls the center of the scene elsewhere.
- **Inventing an action.** Choose a small action that really happened, or plausibly did. An exaggerated gesture can read as false.

## Tips for using draft()

After you talk for about thirty seconds, draft() offers two or three gentle questions. While you speak, stop once and ask yourself, "What was my body doing then?" When an action comes to mind, the peak of the story often gets clearer along with it.

## Try this today

Pick one sentence you wrote recently that has an adjective in it. Recall what the body did in that state and rewrite it as one sentence. Two lines are enough. Read the two versions aloud and check which one is closer to the scene.
