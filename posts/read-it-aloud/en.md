After you write a few lines and read them silently, they usually look fine. Read the same lines aloud, and somewhere you run out of breath, hear the same word twice, or catch yourself skipping a word without meaning to. The eyes forgive; the mouth doesn't. Reading aloud once is the lightest edit there is: no new rules to learn, just your ear.

## The mouth finds what the eyes miss

When you read silently, your eyes run ahead and fill in the gaps on their own. Aloud, you have to go one word at a time, at the speed of speech, so you meet every word. A sentence that is too long runs out of air, a repeated word sounds like an echo, and a stiff phrase you would never say makes your voice go flat. Those are places where a reader may stumble too.

## Three steps for reading and fixing

1. **Read the whole thing once without stopping.** Don't fix anything while you read. Just put a small mark wherever you stumble, take a breath, hear a repeated word, or your voice goes flat.
2. **Look only at the marks.** At each one, ask: "Where would I naturally pause here?" The fix is usually one of three things. Split a long sentence in two, delete the repeated word, or swap the stiff phrase for the words you'd actually say.
3. **Read only the changed spots aloud again.** If it flows, stop there. Don't start rewriting the parts that never caught.

## Example: the elevator in the morning

Before the fix:

> When I got on the elevator that morning, I noticed my neighbor, who always seemed to be in a hurry and whom I had never really talked to, standing there holding a small paper bag that looked like it had something warm in it.

Read aloud, you run out of breath around "in a hurry". Split it where you would breathe:

> I got on the elevator that morning. My neighbor was already there, holding a small paper bag with something warm in it. She always looked busy, and we had never really talked.

Nothing new was added. Only the places to breathe moved, and the pace of reading became natural.

## Keep just three kinds of marks

- **A slash (/)**: where you ran out of breath and had to break. A candidate for splitting the sentence.
- **An underline**: where the same word came back. A candidate for deleting one.
- **A wave**: where it doesn't sound like something you would say. A candidate for putting it in your own words.

If you end up with too many marks, you may have read too fast, so try again more slowly.

## Common stumbling blocks

- **Reading too fast.** When you rush past, the catches go by with it. Read at the speed you'd use to tell a friend.
- **Stopping to fix as you read.** If you stop at every catch, the rhythm breaks and you can't hear the whole piece. Mark it and keep going to the end.
- **Rewriting everything.** The goal is to fix the marked places, not to make every sentence better. Leave the sentences that didn't catch alone.

If you are somewhere crowded, a quiet murmur is fine. Moving your lips and keeping the pace is enough.

## Tips for using draft()

If you have already talked through a story for about thirty seconds, you know how it feels in your own voice. When you look at the written story afterwards, read one paragraph aloud in that same voice. A place that sounds different from the way you told it is exactly where to put a mark.

## Try this today

Pick three sentences you wrote today. A message or a note will do. Read them aloud once, mark where you stumble, and fix only the first mark. The rest can wait until you read it again tomorrow.
