You had a whole day full of things worth telling, and now you're staring at a blinking cursor in the caption box. The words aren't missing because you're a bad writer. You started at the hardest part: the blank page.

## Why talking is easier

Start a sentence with "so, something happened today" to a friend and the story just comes. Someone is listening, you're allowed to be wrong, and you're allowed to pause. Writing is the opposite: the first line feels like it has to be perfect, so you never begin. Flip the order. Say it first, shape it afterwards.

## Thirty seconds is enough

Don't try to say it well. This is perfectly fine:

> Um, so on the way home the subway stopped… and this older woman next to me handed me a tangerine. Just like that. Told me to eat it. And it's weirdly stuck with me.

Stumbling is fine. Broken sentences are fine. Everything you need is already in there: a place (the subway), an event (a tangerine), and the feeling that stayed (it lingers).

## Questions find the real moment

Alone, you get stuck on "what am I even trying to say?" A good editor asks questions at exactly this point. draft() listens to what you said and offers two or three gentle questions. You can ask yourself the same ones when you write by hand:

- What do you remember most clearly from that scene?
- What went through your mind in that moment?
- Looking back, why did it stay with you?

One or two sentences each is plenty. The more concrete, the better: "the tangerine was still warm," or "it had been a while since a stranger was kind to me."

## The order that makes it a story

Raw material reads like a story when it follows this order: hook, rising, peak, reflection, close. With the example above:

> The subway stopped at the most tired hour of my commute. Then the woman beside me held out a tangerine, without a word. It was still warm. That small kindness stayed with me far longer than it should have. Some days, the city is softer than you expect.

It opens with a hook, shows the scene, looks back once at what it meant, and closes short. There isn't a grand sentence anywhere.

## Tips for using draft()

1. **Don't try to say it perfectly.** Mumbling is raw material.
2. **Include one concrete detail.** A time, a place, or who said what is enough.
3. **Answer the questions briefly.** A sentence or two as it comes to you beats a long explanation.
4. **The 11pm version is fine.** The rough version stays with you; only the finished story gets shared, when you choose to share it.

## Try this today

If your caption box is empty right now, close it for a moment and talk through your day for thirty seconds. The story was in you the whole time. It just needed a way out.
