You write a first sentence, delete it, write it again, delete it again, and suddenly an hour has passed. When you try to write well from the very start, judging arrives before writing does. The job of a first version isn't to be good; it's to exist, all the way to the end. A rough piece that reaches the end can be fixed. A piece abandoned halfway has nothing left to fix.

## Why a rough first version helps

Writing and choosing are two different jobs, and doing both at once slows down both. While you write, let things land as they come, and put the choosing off until you're done. Then you have a finished piece in your hands, and only then can you see what needs changing.

## Three steps for a rough first version

1. **Set a small finish line.** Pick an end you can see, like "five sentences" or "ten minutes." When the end is visible, the urge to quit halfway gets smaller.
2. **Keep going without looking back.** When a word won't come, leave a blank, [ ], and move on. Stopping over one word breaks the flow. Don't delete; leave mistakes where they are.
3. **Stop at the finish line and close it without rereading.** First accept that you reached the end. Rereading can wait until later, even tomorrow.

## Example: a thank-you to a neighbor

Say you're writing a message to thank a neighbor. A rough first version looks like this:

> Last week, thank you so much for [ ]. Honestly, it was a hard day, but when I saw the [ ] you left at my door, I smiled. I don't know what to say, but anyway, I wanted to say thank you.

There are blanks and awkward spots. Still, everything you wanted to say is in there. Later you fill in the [ ] and smooth out "anyway." If you had waited to find the perfect thank-you from the start, you might not have written a single line yet.

## So that rough writing doesn't embarrass you

A first version isn't something you show anyone. Think of it as your own working copy, and your mind gets lighter. Treat it like a note in a drawer. Feeling that it's rough isn't a sign that the piece is wrong; it only means you haven't revised it yet.

## Common stumbling blocks

- **Staying on the first sentence.** The first sentence often changes while you write. Put down a placeholder and move on.
- **Fixing as you write.** Writing a line and deleting a line gets you nowhere. Fixing comes after you've written to the end.
- **Comparing it with other writing.** Set someone else's finished piece beside your first version and you'll always look short. The thing to compare with a finished piece is your second version.

## Tips for using draft()

When you talk for about thirty seconds, what you say comes out naturally rough. It doesn't have to be smooth. Answer draft()'s two or three gentle questions and gather a rough first version that way. Polishing comes after.

## Try this today

Pick one short piece you'd like to write today. Set the finish line at "five sentences," leave a [ ] wherever you get stuck, and write all the way through. When you're done, don't reread it; just close it.
