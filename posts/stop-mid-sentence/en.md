People say the hardest part of writing is starting, but how hard it is to start depends on where you stopped yesterday. A piece you closed while stuck, with nothing left to say, feels like a blank screen when you open it again. A piece you left while you still knew what came next picks up the moment you open it. Stopping a little early on purpose isn't laziness; it's a small gift to tomorrow's you.

## Why stopping at a dead end makes things harder

When you're stuck and push on anyway, you get tired, and the memory of closing the file while tired turns into a reluctance to open it again. When you stop while there's still something to say, the wish to write more stays with you. That wish is the best fuel for tomorrow morning.

## Three steps for stopping and picking up

1. **Picture the next line.** At the spot where you're writing, ask yourself once: "What comes next?" If something comes to mind, this is your place to stop. If nothing does, write a little more.
2. **Keep that line as a note, not as writing.** At the very bottom, jot something short like "Next: my sister lifts the piano lid." Holding yourself back from writing it out is the trick.
3. **Close it in the middle of a sentence.** If you can, leave one sentence unfinished. An unfinished sentence almost asks to be finished when you reopen it.

## Example: the piano story

Say this is all you wrote today:

> My sister sat down at the old piano without a word. When she lifted the lid, the dust

The sentence isn't finished, but you already know what comes next. Add one note below it:

> Next: the smell of dust, and the first note she played

Tomorrow you can pick up right at "the dust," and the note tells you where the piece is going. Starting from here weighs far less than starting from an empty screen.

## When you don't want to stop

On a good day you'll want to keep going, and that's fine. But once you've written as much as you meant to today, try stopping at the point where it flows best. A piece left while there's still energy in it picks up best the next day.

## Common stumbling blocks

- **Writing the note too long.** If you write out the whole next line, it has already become the piece. One line is enough for a note.
- **Stopping while stuck.** If you can't picture the next line and you close the file anyway, you're simply stuck. Write a few more lines, or leave a note about what is blocking you.
- **Losing the note.** Keep the note in the same place as the writing. If you put it somewhere else, you won't find it tomorrow.

## Tips for using draft()

After you talk for about thirty seconds, draft() offers two or three gentle questions. When you feel the urge to answer every last one, leave one on purpose. One unanswered question can be the thread that pulls tomorrow's story along.

## Try this today

Pick one thing you're writing now, or a note. Stop where you can picture what comes next, and write "Next: …" in one line at the bottom. Tomorrow, start from that line.
