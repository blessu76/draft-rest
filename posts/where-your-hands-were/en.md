When we remember a moment, we tend to remember faces and words and forget the hands. Yet hands are busy in almost every scene: holding a cup, folding a receipt, gripping a phone. They are also honest. A person can say "I'm fine" while their hands tear a napkin into strips. Putting hands into a scene lets the reader read the mood without being told.

## Why hands carry so much

Hands are the part of the body that is always doing something small. Small actions are specific, and specific details are what make a reader believe a scene. Hands also tend to move when the voice stays calm, so they show what the voice is hiding.

## Three steps to put hands in a scene

1. **Find the hands in your memory.** Ask where they were: on a table, in a pocket, around a cup.
2. **Give them one small task.** Turning a ring, pressing a thumbnail into a palm, smoothing a tablecloth.
3. **Let the hands agree or disagree with the words.** Either choice tells the reader something. Then say nothing more about it.

## Example: the phone call

Say you first wrote this:

> She told me she was fine, but she sounded nervous.

Now rewrite it as a scene:

> "I'm fine," she said. Her free hand kept folding the corner of the envelope, flatten, fold, flatten, until the paper went soft as cloth.

The nervousness is never named, and it is easy to see.

## Common stumbling blocks

- **Using hands as decoration.** Don't add a gesture to every line. One well-placed action is stronger than five.
- **Explaining the gesture.** "Which showed she was nervous" cancels the effect. Trust the action.
- **Choosing a cliché.** Clenched fists and trembling fingers are worn out. Look for the odd, true action you really saw.

## Tips for using draft()

After you talk for about thirty seconds, draft() offers two or three gentle questions. While you tell your story, pause once and picture the other person's hands, or your own. Whatever they were doing is often a detail you would not have thought to say out loud.

## Try this today

Pick one conversation from today. Write where the hands were and what they did in one or two lines, without any feeling word. Read it aloud and check whether the mood still comes through.
