"Nothing really happened today." The days when that sentence comes out most are the days with the most stories hiding in them. A day without a big event isn't a day without a story. It just means the eye for small things hasn't switched on yet.

## You don't need a big event

The pieces people read to the end often have no dramatic event at all: the sunset on the way home, a neighbor whose eyes met yours in the elevator, a changed menu at a shop you always go to. What makes a story isn't the size of the event but the gaze of the person who noticed it.

## Three questions for an ordinary day

1. **Was anything different from usual, even one thing?** The route, the weather, a person, a sound, anything.
2. **Did anyone say anything?** Even a very short sentence becomes a scene.
3. **Did your body react before you did?** A laugh, a slower step, a sigh.

An answer to just one of the three is plenty of material for a story.

## Example: an uneventful Tuesday

> Nothing really happened… oh, but when I came back from lunch, the flower shop that's always closed was open. The owner was putting plants out front, and when she saw me she said, "Nice weather today." I just liked that.

Everything is in there: something different (the closed shop was open), a line (Nice weather today), and a reaction (I just liked that). Shaped up, it becomes:

> The flower shop that is always closed was open today. The owner, setting plants out front, looked at me and said, "Nice weather today." A throwaway greeting nudged the temperature of my day up a little.

## Common stumbling blocks

- **Giving up with "there's nothing to write."** It's not that there's nothing; the bar for what counts is set too high.
- **Looking for the meaning first.** Meaning follows once the scene is written.
- **Comparing with someone else's day.** Next to a glamorous day, yours will always look dull.

## Tips for using draft()

draft() helps you find the real moment in what you said with two or three gentle questions. So starting from "nothing happened" is fine. Just talk for thirty seconds about the small things from today, in any order. While you talk, scenes you didn't know you had often pop out.

## Try this today

Go back over your day and look for just one thing that was different from usual. When you find it, say it out loud in a single sentence. A day with nothing in it starts becoming a story with that one sentence.
