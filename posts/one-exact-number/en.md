Sentences like "I waited a long time" or "it was expensive" don't build a picture in the reader's mind. Put just one number into the same scene. "I waited forty minutes," "it cost seven dollars and fifty cents": an exact number holds a scene firmly in place, because a number reads like evidence rather than explanation.

## Why one number makes a scene clearer

A vague phrase is read at a different size by each reader. A number is read at the same size by everyone. A number also shows how closely the person was counting. Someone who counted exactly was bothered by the thing, and that one line carries the feeling without naming it.

## Three steps to add a number

1. **Find one vague word in your scene.** "Long," "a lot," "a little" and "far" are good candidates.
2. **Replace it with a price, a count, a time or a distance.** If you have no real memory of it, a believable number you choose yourself is fine.
3. **Add a few words on how the character came to know the number.** Did they see the receipt, keep checking the clock, count on their fingers?

## Example: at the meeting place

Say you first wrote this:

> He waited a long time, but his friend never came.

Now rewrite it with a number:

> He woke his phone screen for the thirty-first time. Forty minutes had passed since the agreed hour. Beside the cold coffee he read the four-fifty on the receipt once more for no reason.

Without the word "long," the size of the wait comes through.

## Common stumbling blocks

- **Using too many numbers.** One per scene is enough. More numbers make it read like a report.
- **Writing the number and stopping.** Always connect it to how the character learned it. A number has to be tied to a person.
- **Hiding behind round figures.** "Nine" is much firmer than "ten or so."

## Tips for using draft()

After you talk for about thirty seconds, draft() offers two or three gentle questions. While you speak, stop once and ask yourself, "How many times was it? How much was it?" When the number comes back, the peak of the story often settles into something more concrete.

## Try this today

Pick one thing from today that you described as "long" or "a lot." Write one line that turns it into a number and one line on how you came to know it. Read them aloud and check whether the scene now stands more clearly in front of you.
