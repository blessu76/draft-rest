Plenty of good stories get spoiled by their last line, something like "and I learned how precious small kindnesses are." It's true, but it takes away the part the reader was going to feel for themselves.

## Why morals land softly

Readers feel more when they notice something in a scene than when they're told. Here are two ways to close the same story.

> That day I realized how precious people's kindness is.

> The tangerine stayed warm in my hand all the way home.

The first states a conclusion; the second leaves a scene. The second lingers because the reader fills in the meaning.

## Three ways to close on an echo

1. **End on an image.** Place one visible thing at the very end: coffee going cold, a door closing, a window lit up.
2. **Step back for one short look.** Don't unspool the feeling. Write one sentence: "The city was softer than I expected that day."
3. **Return to your opening line.** Bring back a phrase from the hook in a slightly changed form and the story closes in a circle.

You don't need all three. Pick one.

## Reflection vs. moral

A reflection says how you feel. A moral teaches the reader.

- Reflection: "That one sentence stayed with me for an odd amount of time."
- Moral: "We should all be kinder to one another."

A reflection needs one sentence, and it sounds most natural when the subject is "I."

## Finding your ending in what you said

The raw version you spoke already holds ending material. Look at what you let slip at the very end.

> …so, yeah, I guess it was a good day.

Take the feeling in "I guess it was a good day," add a single scene, and you have an ending. You don't have to manufacture meaning.

## Tips for using draft()

When draft() asks something like "Looking back, why did it stay with you?", answer briefly and honestly. It doesn't need to be a grand realization: "it just keeps coming back to me" is enough. That honest line becomes the material for the sentence that closes the story.

## Try this today

Pick the last sentence of something you wrote recently, delete the lesson, and replace it with a scene or a one-line reflection. The shorter the final sentence, the longer the echo.
