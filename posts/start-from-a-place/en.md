Not sure what your story is about yet? Start with where it happens. A place is easier to describe than a feeling, and it quietly holds the people, the sounds and the small events that make a story. Pick one place, and let it lead you into the piece.

## Why a place is a good starting point

A feeling is hard to point at, but a place can be pointed at. You can stand in it, look to the left and write down what is there. Once the place is on the page, the reader has somewhere to stand too, and the story has room to happen. You don't have to invent a plot first. You only have to remember where you were.

## Three steps from a place to a story

1. **Choose one small place.** Not a city or a country: a bench, a stairwell, the corner of a kitchen. The smaller it is, the more of it you can actually see.
2. **List three things you could see or hear there.** Write them plainly, like a humming fridge, a chipped cup, or a neighbor's radio through the wall. Then pick the one that surprises you a little.
3. **Add one thing that happened there.** One moment is enough, such as the night the lights went out or the time someone stayed longer than planned. Write it right after your three details.

## Example: the laundromat on the corner

Say you want to write about a laundromat you used to visit. Without a place, it might read like this:

> It was a quiet old laundromat, and I spent a lot of time there thinking about things.

With the place and one moment, it changes:

> The wall clock ran four minutes slow, and every dryer door opened with a puff of warm soap smell. One winter night the power cut out mid-cycle, and the five of us sitting there started talking about what we had been putting off.

The second version tells you where you are, and a story is already starting.

## Common stumbling blocks

- **Picking a place that is too big.** "My hometown" holds too much. Shrink it to one corner you could draw from memory.
- **Describing without stopping.** If the list of details keeps growing, you are writing a catalog. Stop at three and move on to what happened.
- **Choosing a place where nothing happened.** If nothing ever happened there, pick another place, or ask yourself what you were waiting for while you stood there.

## Tips for using draft()

draft() takes about thirty seconds of what you say, adds two or three gentle questions, and shapes your answers into a story with a hook, rising action, a peak, a reflection and a close. Try opening your thirty seconds with the place, such as "I'm at the bus stop by the river." The questions that follow often ask about what happened there.

## Try this today

Pick one small place you were in this week. Write three things you could see or hear there, then one thing that happened. Read the four lines in order, and see whether the place has already started telling you a story. If it hasn't, add one more small detail and read again.
