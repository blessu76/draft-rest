Someone asks, "How did it feel?" and the quickest answer arrives at once: "sad," "happy," "strange." These words are true, but they are also doors that close. Once you say "it was sad," the listener nods and the story is over. A scene keeps the door open. It lets the other person stand in the room with you and feel it on their own.

## Why a feeling word ends the story

A feeling word is a summary. It compresses everything you remember into one label, and labels are shared by thousands of other stories. A scene is specific to you. When the listener sees the same cup, the same doorway, they do not need to be told what it meant.

## Three steps to answer with a scene

1. **Say the feeling word once, then set it aside.** Write "sad" at the top of the page if you must, and promise yourself not to use it again.
2. **Ask what was in front of you.** Not what you felt, but what you saw, heard or held in that moment.
3. **Write that one image and stop.** Resist the urge to add "and that is why I felt sad." The image is the answer.

## Example: after the visit

Say your first answer was this:

> After they left, I felt lonely.

Now answer with a scene instead:

> After they left, I stood in the doorway holding two cups. I had poured the second one without thinking, and the tea was still too hot to drink.

The word "lonely" never appears, yet the second cup says it more gently than the word could.

## Common stumbling blocks

- **Swapping one feeling word for another.** "Heartbroken" is not a scene. If the sentence still names an emotion, keep digging for the object.
- **Choosing a grand image.** A sunset or a storm is borrowed. The small thing you really noticed is more convincing.
- **Explaining the image afterwards.** If you add a sentence that says what the cup meant, you have closed the door again.

## Tips for using draft()

After you talk for about thirty seconds, draft() offers two or three gentle questions. When one of them asks how you felt, try answering with what you could see. Speak a single image aloud, and let draft() help you shape it into a short story.

## Try this today

Think of one moment from this week and write down the feeling word. Cross it out. Then write one or two lines describing what was in front of you, and read them aloud. Notice whether the feeling is still there without the word.
