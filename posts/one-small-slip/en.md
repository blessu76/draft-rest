A day in which everything went smoothly tends to look flat on the page. A real day almost always holds one small slip: a spilled cup, a wrong name, keys left behind. We usually want to forget such moments, but in writing they are among the best ways to make a scene feel real.

## Why a small slip gives a scene strength

A slip shows the person. Habits and feelings that stay hidden while everything goes to plan come out when something slips. Readers also tend to find their own day in a story that is slightly off rather than a perfect one. A small mistake leaves a place for the reader to sit beside you.

## Three steps to leave a slip in

1. **Recall one small slip from today.** It doesn't have to be big. A misspoken word or a wrong guess about the time is enough.
2. **Write it in one line, just as it happened.** Leave out excuses and judgments. Write only what happened.
3. **Add what you did in that moment.** Whether you laughed, pretended not to notice or hurried to fix it, one sentence will do.

## Example: the name read backwards

Say this is what happened today:

> At a gathering, I said hello to someone I'd just met.

Now leave a slip in:

> The moment I took their card, I read the name backwards. They raised an eyebrow for a second, said "It happens a lot," and smiled. I took the card again with both hands.

No excuse and no regret is written, yet the temperature of that moment comes through.

## Common stumbling blocks

- **Inflating the slip.** The smaller it is, the more believable. Don't try to invent a big accident.
- **Ending with self-criticism.** If you close with "I felt so stupid," the scene turns into a verdict. Write only what was done.
- **Mocking someone else's mistake.** A slip works best when it adds warmth to a scene. It is better not to make anyone the butt of the joke.

## Tips for using draft()

After you talk for about thirty seconds, draft() offers two or three gentle questions. While you speak, stop once and ask yourself, "Did anything go slightly off that day?" When the moment comes to mind, the peak of the story and its ending often get clearer along with it.

## Try this today

Recall one small slip from today. Write what happened in one line and what you did in that moment in another. Two lines are enough. Read them aloud and check whether you can feel the air of that moment.
