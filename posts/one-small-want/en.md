A piece that feels as if nothing happens is often missing a want. The moment a person wants something, even slightly, the scene starts to move. It doesn't have to be a grand goal. Wanting the last open seat or hoping for a phone call is enough. Write one line about what the person in your story wanted in that moment.

## Why a small want sets a scene moving

Once a want appears, the reader naturally watches to see whether it will come true. The scene gets a direction. A want also shows who the person is. Two people in the same café make two different scenes if one wants quiet and the other is waiting for someone.

## Three steps to add a small want

1. **Write what the person wanted in that moment, in one line.** It can be tiny: "I wanted the seat by the window." If the story is yours, recall what you wanted then.
2. **Name one thing that stood in the way.** A person who sat down first, too little time, a moment of hesitation: any small obstacle in front of the want will do.
3. **Add what they did about it.** Whether they gave up, waited or found another way, one sentence is enough.

## Example: the seat by the window

Say this is what happened today:

> I went to a café. The window seat was taken.

Now add a want:

> On rainy days I wanted the window seat. Someone had a laptop open there. I took my coffee slowly and waited by the door for their cup to empty.

Without the word "disappointed," the size of the want comes through.

## Common stumbling blocks

- **Making the want too big.** Something like "I wanted to change my life" can read as false. A size that could play out within a day works better.
- **Writing the want and stopping.** With a want but nothing in its way, the scene doesn't move. Always add one obstacle.
- **Ending with an explanation of the result.** Showing the motion of giving up, in one line, tends to stay longer than writing "in the end I gave up."

## Tips for using draft()

After you talk for about thirty seconds, draft() offers two or three gentle questions. While you speak, stop once and ask yourself, "What did I want then?" When a want comes to mind, the flow of the story and its peak often get clearer along with it.

## Try this today

Pick one short moment from today. Write what was wanted in one line, what stood in the way in another, and what was done about it in a third. Three lines are enough. Read them aloud once and check whether the scene moves.
