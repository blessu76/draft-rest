Has someone asked "How was your day?" and all you could say was "fine"? Try to explain a whole day and you won't know where to start. A story doesn't begin with a day. It begins with one scene.

## One scene, not the whole day

Summarize morning to night and there's so much material that nothing is left. Ask this instead: "What stayed in my head the longest today?" It can be your lunch order or a single sentence you overheard in the elevator. The smaller the scene, the easier it is to say.

## Say just three things

Thirty seconds is shorter than it sounds, so don't get greedy. Say only these three:

1. **Where it was.** One line about the place or situation.
2. **What happened.** One or two sentences.
3. **How you felt.** One line about what stayed with you.

For example:

> It was the convenience store near my office, and my card got declined. There was a line behind me, and the clerk just smiled and said, "Take your time." That one sentence took the edge off my whole day.

All three are there, and it doesn't matter if the words get messy along the way.

## Common slip-ups

- **Starting with explanation.** "I actually go to that store a lot, so…" pushes the scene far down. Start with what happened.
- **Rushing to a lesson.** One line of feeling is enough. The questions will lead you to a conclusion later.
- **Stopping to redo it.** If you stumble, don't restart. Just keep going.

## Practicing with draft()

In draft(), once you've talked for about thirty seconds, two or three gentle questions follow. So you don't have to say it well the first time. As practice, tell one scene at the same time every day. As yesterday's scene and today's pile up, speaking gets easier. The raw version stays with you; only the finished story gets shared, when you choose.

## Today's homework

Pick one scene that comes to mind right now and say it out loud for thirty seconds: place, event, feeling, in that order. A timer helps. When you finish, you'll be surprised. You had more to say than you thought.
