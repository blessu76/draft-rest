Look back over a day and even when nothing big happened, there is usually one small kindness: someone who held a door, an umbrella handed over without a word, a few seconds a stranger waited at the register. Such moments are forgotten quickly, but leave one line about it in a story and warmth spreads through the whole scene. You don't need a grand, moving moment, only a small and concrete one.

## Why a small kindness can change a story

Kindness shows best in small actions rather than big words. Reading them, the reader senses what kind of world the character lives in. And the awkwardness or gratitude of the person who received it is hard to hide, which makes the character feel more real.

## Three steps to write a small kindness

1. **Write who did what in one line.** Only the action: "The person beside me lifted their bag onto their lap."
2. **Add one bodily reaction from the person who received it.** Did they bow their head, smile for no reason, swallow their words?
3. **Add one sentence on what changed afterward.** If the air of the day loosened a little, show that loosening.

## Example: the bus on a rainy day

Say you first wrote this:

> The bus was crowded. Someone kindly gave up a seat.

Now rewrite it as a scene:

> The bus was crowded. The student in front stood up without a word and slid the pole toward me. I said thank you twice, quietly, and only after setting my wet umbrella against my toes did my shoulders drop.

Without the word "kindly," the kindness is visible.

## Common stumbling blocks

- **Dressing the kindness up too much.** Phrases like "an angel of a person" make it feel distant. A small action is more believable.
- **Writing only the helper and stopping.** Always add the receiver's reaction. A kindness becomes a story only with a reaction.
- **Closing with a lesson.** Cut lines like "the world is still warm" and stop on the action.

## Tips for using draft()

After you talk for about thirty seconds, draft() offers two or three gentle questions. While you speak, look back once and ask yourself, "Who did what for me then?" When the small kindness comes back, the ending of the story often settles more warmly.

## Try this today

Recall one small kindness someone showed you today. Write what that person did in one line and how you reacted in another. Two lines are enough. Read them aloud and check whether the gratitude comes through even though you never wrote "thank you."
