Short stories usually go blurry for one reason: too many people are standing in them. "We had a team dinner and everyone was laughing and talking" is true, but the reader can't see anyone. Pick one person and the picture comes into focus.

## One face outlasts ten

People don't remember groups; they remember faces. In a story with five names and not a single line of dialogue, nobody feels real. When one person does one thing, the others can stay in the background and the scene still feels full.

## Three small questions about that person

1. **What were their hands doing?** Stirring a cup, folding a receipt, holding a phone upside down.
2. **What did they say?** One sentence, word for word if you can.
3. **What did only you notice?** A passing glance, a short pause, a laugh a beat too late.

You don't need all three. One good detail is enough to make a person stand up and start walking around the page.

## Example: a team dinner

> We had a team dinner. Everyone was laughing and talking, and it was fun.

Now put one person in the middle:

> At the team dinner, Minji kept folding her napkin into smaller and smaller squares. When the others burst out laughing, she said quietly, "I'm just glad everyone came." Then she went back to folding.

Same evening, but now the reader has been to that table.

## Common stumbling blocks

- **Listing everyone who was there.** Name the one person who matters and leave the rest as "the others."
- **Labeling instead of showing.** "She's shy" is an explanation; the hands folding the napkin are a scene.
- **Judging the person.** Write what they did and leave the verdict out. The reader will reach it.

## Tips for using draft()

With draft() you talk for about thirty seconds, and the two or three gentle questions that follow can help you notice who in your story actually stayed with you. If you mentioned several people, see which one got the most of your words. That person is often your main character.

## Try this today

Think of one conversation you had today. Pick one person in it and write down one thing they did with their hands or one sentence they said. Start the story there.
