Someone scrolling decides on the first line whether to keep reading. That's why the first sentence of a story, the hook, matters. It doesn't have to be grand. Simply dropping into the middle of a scene changes the whole piece.

## What happens when you open with explanation

Plenty of us write first lines like this:

> Today I met a friend I hadn't seen in a while and we went to a café.

It isn't wrong, but it gives no hint of what's about to happen. Start the same day from a scene and it becomes:

> My friend called my name across the café the second she opened the door.

The reader is standing inside the scene right away. Same content, different starting point.

## Three ways to build a hook

1. **Start with an action.** "I opened the door and…" or "The moment I picked up the phone…" begin at a moment when someone is moving.
2. **Show one specific thing.** A visible detail like "lights half off" or "the coffee had gone cold" holds a sentence in place.
3. **Drop in a small surprise.** "That day I was carrying two umbrellas." makes the reader wonder why.

One of the three is plenty. Cram in several and it just gets cluttered.

## Finding the hook in what you said

The raw version you speak often has a hook hiding in it already.

> Um, so I went to the bakery and it was basically closing time… and the lights were half off…

Here, "the lights were half off" is the hook. It becomes a first line like "The bakery lights were already half off when I got there." Pull the part of what you said where the scene was clearest to the front.

## Openings to avoid

- **A long run-up of weather or time.** Trim it unless it's part of the story.
- **Summarizing with "Today was a day of…".** A summary fits at the end.
- **Opening only with a question.** If a scene doesn't follow right away, it sounds empty.

## Tips for using draft()

draft() shapes what you say into a story with a structure from hook to close. So instead of agonizing over the first line yourself, add one more moment from your talk where the scene was vivid. When draft() asks its questions, answer with the sharpest detail you remember in a single line. That detail often becomes the starting point.

## Try this today

Take the first line of something you wrote recently and rewrite it as a scene instead of an explanation. Changing just a few words changes where the reader is standing.
