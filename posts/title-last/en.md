Have you ever sat at the title box for a long time? Naming a piece you haven't written yet is hard, and that makes sense. A title needs you to know what the piece turned out to be. For a short piece, it is usually easier to write the title last. Write first, and let the finished piece tell you its name.

## Why the title is easier at the end

A title written before the piece is a promise. If you label it "A Special Day," the piece strains to keep that promise and the explanations grow. After you finish, you can see what actually remains on the page. Name that, and the title never gets ahead of the writing.

## Three steps to find the title

1. **Leave the title blank and write to the end.** "Untitled" is enough for now. Rough is fine; just get to the last line.
2. **Look for what stays with you.** Among a word that appears twice or more, an object in a scene, or one line of speech, pick whatever you would still remember tomorrow.
3. **Write three candidates and keep one.** Keep each to about six words, then read the title and the first sentence together aloud and keep the one that sounds most natural.

## Example: the umbrella at the bus stop

Say you are writing about a rainy day when a stranger handed you an umbrella at a bus stop. The title written before the piece might be:

> A Kind Stranger

After finishing, you notice that a blue umbrella with one bent rib keeps showing up. So the title changes:

> The Blue Umbrella With One Bent Rib

The first title gives the conclusion away. The second makes a reader curious enough to step into the first sentence.

## Common stumbling blocks

- **Using the moral as the title.** A title like "The Power of Small Kindness" tells the ending before anyone reads. The name of an object or scene stays with a reader longer.
- **Making it too long.** Once a title runs past one short sentence, it has become a summary. Keep only a few of the most concrete words.
- **A title that doesn't match the piece.** If a catchy phrase clashes with the mood of the writing, readers feel tricked. Use words that actually appear in the piece.

## Tips for using draft()

draft() takes about thirty seconds of talking, adds two or three gentle questions, and shapes your answers into a story with a hook, a rising part, a peak, a reflection and a close. Read the close and underline the clearest scene or word. A title candidate often comes from there.

## Try this today

Pick a short piece you wrote a few days ago and cover its title. Choose one thing that stays with you, then write three title candidates of about six words each. Read each title together with the first sentence aloud, and keep the least awkward one.
